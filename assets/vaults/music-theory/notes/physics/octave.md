An **octave** is the interval between a [[frequency]] and its double — a **2:1 ratio**. Notes an octave apart sound so alike that we call them "the same note, higher or lower": they share a letter [[note_name|name]] and belong to the same [[pitch_class|pitch class]]. Two waves, one exactly double the other's frequency:

![[notes/resources/images/octave_sine.svg]]

Hear it — two C's, one octave apart:

![[notes/resources/audio/octave.mp3]]

**More examples.** The reference pitch A4 = 440 Hz, doubled, gives A5 = 880 Hz — one octave up:

![[notes/resources/audio/octave_a4_a5.mp3]]

And because *each* octave is another doubling, you can climb them in a stack — here A2 → A3 → A4 → A5 is 110 → 220 → 440 → 880 Hz: four A's, three octaves, every step twice the frequency of the one before it:

![[notes/resources/audio/octave_stack_a.mp3]]

The octave is the *period* of the whole naming system: it spans **12 [[semitone|semitones]]**, and the names cycle back every octave. That climbing stack is exactly why the frequency→[[pitch]] map is **logarithmic**— equal musical steps (octaves) are equal *ratios* (×2), not equal hertz gaps.

**Try it:** [[exercises/octave_up]] — enter the note one octave above C4 and get feedback that ties the name, the octave number, and the frequency together.
