Every directory before this one assumes some version of a key center — even the most chromatic harmony in [[chromatic_harmony/Chromatic_harmony|chromatic harmony]] is still harmony orbiting a tonic it eventually returns to. The twentieth century is largely the story of composers deciding that assumption was optional. Debussy and Ravel don't abandon tonal chords, they abandon tonal FUNCTION — a major seventh or a whole-tone collection used for its color rather than its need to resolve, closer to how a painter might use the physics from [[Nice_And_Ugly]] as raw material than as a rulebook.

Schoenberg's serialism is the opposite move: not a loosening of tonal rules but a replacement of them with a different, equally strict system — a fixed ordered row of pitches generating the entire piece, motivated by the sense that tonal harmony had exhausted its own possibilities after Wagner and late Romanticism pushed chromaticism about as far as it could go without breaking. Set theory is the analytical toolkit built to describe that music on its own terms, since triad-and-Roman-numeral analysis has nothing to say about a piece with no key.

Minimalism, decades later, is almost a reaction against both: strip harmonic motion down to almost nothing and let repetition, phase, and gradual process carry the piece instead — Steve Reich's phase pieces (the same phase-shifting idea behind this vault's own [[loom]]) are a direct example of process replacing harmonic narrative as music's organizing structure.

**Listen for it:** put a Debussy prelude, a Schoenberg piano piece, and a Steve Reich phase piece back to back — three completely different answers to the same 20th-century question: if tonality is optional, what organizes the piece instead?
