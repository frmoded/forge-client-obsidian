The **mandolin** is a small plucked string instrument with a bowl or flat back and a fretted neck. It has four **courses** (pairs) of metal strings, so eight strings in all, and each pair is played as one note. It is tuned in fifths, like a violin: from the lowest pair up, G, D, A, E (G3 D4 A4 E5). A modern neck has about 20 frets, and the scale length is about 13 inches (330 mm).

Because the tuning is in fifths, each pair of neighbouring open courses is a perfect fifth apart (see [[Intervals]]).

Try it: click a position to hold it, then play the notes together, ascending or descending; switch the tuning to hear how the same frets sit in other tunings.

## History

The mandolin descends from the lute family, through the small Italian mandola and mandolino of the 17th and 18th centuries. The Neapolitan mandolin gave the instrument its modern form.
