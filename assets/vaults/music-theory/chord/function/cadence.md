# Cadence

A **cadence** is how a progression punctuates — its resolution point, the harmonic equivalent of a punctuation mark:

- **Authentic** (V→I) — a full stop. Complete resolution, dominant to tonic.
- **Plagal** (IV→I) — the "amen" cadence. Softer than authentic, predominant straight to tonic.
- **Half** (anything→V) — a comma. Ends hanging on the dominant, unresolved.
- **Deceptive** (V→vi) — a feint. Sets up an authentic resolution, then swerves to vi instead.

Run [[chord_progression]] four times with `key_name="C"`, `mode_name="major"` and these endings, listening for the difference each makes: `["I", "IV", "V", "I"]` (authentic), `["I", "V", "IV", "I"]` (plagal), `["I", "IV", "V"]` (half — stop right there, don't add the I), `["I", "IV", "V", "vi"]` (deceptive). Same setup every time; only the last chord (or two) changes, and the ending is the whole story.

Cadences are how you know a [[chord_progression]] is "over" — or, in the deceptive case, how a composer plays with your expectation of when it's over.
