# Harmonic Function

**Harmonic function** is the *role* a chord plays in a key, not just its label. The seven [[diatonic_chord|diatonic chords]] group into three jobs:

- **Tonic** (I and vi) — home, rest, stability.
- **Dominant** (V and vii°) — tension, wants to resolve to tonic.
- **Predominant / subdominant** (IV and ii) — the approach, sets up the dominant.

A progression is motion between these three jobs, not just a list of chord names — which is why V and vii° feel interchangeable (both dominant) even though they're built on different scale degrees.

Run [[chord_progression]] with `progression=["I", "IV", "V", "I"]` — tonic, predominant, dominant, tonic — and listen for the tension building through the middle two chords before it resolves back home on the last I. Then try `["I", "vi", "IV", "V"]`: same functional shape (tonic-ish, predominant, dominant), different literal chords.

How a progression chooses to *end* — which function it lands on — is exactly what [[cadence]] is about.
