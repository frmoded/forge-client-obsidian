# Chord Tones vs. the Rest

A chord's **chord tones** are the notes actually in the chord — for a triad, degrees 1, 3, and 5; for a [[seventh_chord]], add the 7th. Everything a melody plays that *isn't* a chord tone is a **non-chord tone**: a passing tone stepping between two chord tones, a suspension held over from the previous chord before it resolves down, a neighbor tone stepping away and back. Non-chord tones are decoration — they create motion and tension precisely because they *don't* belong to the underlying harmony.

This is the hinge between melody and harmony: a melody note is either confirming the chord underneath it (a chord tone, stable) or pulling against it (a non-chord tone, wanting to resolve). Run [[build_triad]] with `tonic="C4"`, `quality="major"` to hear the three chord tones of C major in isolation — C, E, G. Any melody note that ISN'T one of those three, played over a C major chord, is a non-chord tone doing decorative work.

[[voicing]] is about how chord tones get arranged; this note is about the boundary between "in the chord" and "not."
