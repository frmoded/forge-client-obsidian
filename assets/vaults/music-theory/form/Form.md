How a piece organizes itself above the level of a single phrase: the shapes a melody's phrases combine into, and the larger sectional forms pieces are built from.

- [[sentence_period]] — two ways a pair of phrases can relate: a period (statement, then a varied answer) versus a sentence (a short idea, repeated, then pushed forward).
- [[phrase_contrast]] — what makes two consecutive phrases feel like a question and answer instead of two unrelated ideas.
- [[binary_ternary_form]] — two-part (AB) and three-part (ABA) sectional forms, the building blocks under most longer pieces.
- [[sonata_form]] and [[rondo_form]] — the two classic large-scale forms built from those smaller sectional blocks.
- [[pop_song_form]] — verse/chorus/bridge, the recognizable modern descendant of the same sectional logic.

See [[form_history]] for how these forms took shape.
