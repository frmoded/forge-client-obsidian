# Semitone (half step)

A **semitone** (or half step) is the smallest interval in standard tuning: **1/12 of an [[octave]]**. It's the quantization grid everything else is built on — hear the tightest step there is, C up to C♯:

![[note/resources/audio/semitone.mp3]]

On the staff, that's the closest two notes ever get to being distinguishable — same line or space, or an adjacent one, with an [[accidental]] doing the work:

![[note/resources/images/semitone_score.svg]]

On guitar, a semitone is simpler to see than to read: it's exactly **one fret** — any note, one fret up on the same string, is one semitone higher, no exceptions. That's visible directly on the full fretboard map in [[guitar_note_names]]: any two adjacent columns are always a semitone apart. Compare that to piano, where the size of a visual "step" between adjacent keys varies — sometimes a semitone, sometimes a whole step, depending on whether a black key sits between them — see [[piano_note_names]].

In equal temperament each semitone is a [[frequency]] ratio of **2^(1/12) ≈ 1.0595**. Every scale, chord, and interval is just a count of these steps — a whole step is two semitones, an octave is twelve.
