With the spelling rule from [[spelling and jargon]] in hand, you can start bending the major scale.

Play the major scale but start on a different note of it, and you get a **mode**. Same notes, new home base, different feel. There are seven: Ionian, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian and Locrian. Ionian is plain old major. The rest are its cousins.

Aeolian is the one you'll meet most. It's the sixth mode of major, better known as **natural minor**, and it tends to sound sad or dark. Natural minor runs W-H-W-W-H-W-W.

Minor has two relatives with an agenda. **Harmonic minor** raises the 7th note so the ending pulls hard toward home. **Melodic minor** raises both the 6th and 7th going up. Coming down, classical practice lowers them again, while jazz keeps them raised both ways. The player has an Up/Down switch so you can hear the difference.

Try it: pick a tonic, flip between natural, harmonic and melodic minor, and set the switch to Up and then Down.

> [!note]- Structural formula
> Natural minor: W-H-W-W-H-W-W, in half steps 2 1 2 2 1 2 2.
> Harmonic minor: 2 1 2 2 1 3 1 (raised 7th).
> Melodic minor going up: 2 1 2 2 2 2 1 (raised 6th and 7th).
> Modes of C major, each starting on the next white key: Ionian from C, Dorian from D, Phrygian from E, Lydian from F, Mixolydian from G, Aeolian from A, Locrian from B.
