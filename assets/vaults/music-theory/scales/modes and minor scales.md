With the spelling rule from [[scales jargon]] in hand, you can start bending the major scale.

Play the major scale but start on a different note of it, and you get a **mode**. Same notes, new home base, different feel. There are seven: Ionian, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian and Locrian. The worked examples all start on C, so you can watch the recipe change and nothing else. A half step is the next piano key (black keys count) or the next fret.

## The seven modes

Each line gives the recipe, how the mode differs from major, and the notes from C.

- **Ionian** is major itself: W-W-H-W-W-W-H. C D E F G A B.
- **Dorian**: W-H-W-W-W-H-W. Major with the 3rd and 7th lowered. C D E♭ F G A B♭.
- **Phrygian**: H-W-W-W-H-W-W. Major with the 2nd, 3rd, 6th and 7th lowered. C D♭ E♭ F G A♭ B♭.
- **Lydian**: W-W-W-H-W-W-H. Major with the 4th raised. C D E F♯ G A B.
- **Mixolydian**: W-W-H-W-W-H-W. Major with the 7th lowered. C D E F G A B♭.
- **Aeolian**: W-H-W-W-H-W-W. Major with the 3rd, 6th and 7th lowered, which makes it natural minor. C D E♭ F G A♭ B♭.
- **Locrian**: H-W-W-H-W-W-W. Major with the 2nd, 3rd, 5th, 6th and 7th lowered: five notes down, the most of any mode. C D♭ E♭ F G♭ A♭ B♭.

Another way in: take a major scale and start on its 2nd note for Dorian, its 3rd for Phrygian, its 4th for Lydian, its 5th for Mixolydian, its 6th for Aeolian, its 7th for Locrian. Same notes, new address. To check any mode, count the half steps against its recipe and give every note its own letter.

## Three minors

Aeolian is natural minor, and it has its own page: [[natural minor scale]]. Minor has two relatives with an agenda.

**Harmonic minor** raises the 7th of natural minor, so the ending pulls hard toward home: W-H-W-W-H, then a step and a half, then H. From C it's C D E♭ F G A♭ B. The gap between A♭ and B is three half steps, an augmented second, and that's the signature. Next to major, it has the 3rd and 6th lowered.

**Melodic minor** raises both the 6th and 7th going up: W-H-W-W-W-W-H. From C: C D E♭ F G A B. Next to major, only the 3rd is lowered. Coming down, classical practice goes back to natural minor (C B♭ A♭ G F E♭ D C), while jazz keeps the raised notes both ways.

To see any scale on a keyboard and a staff, use the player on [[Scales]].

> [!note]- Structural formula
> Natural minor: W-H-W-W-H-W-W, in half steps 2 1 2 2 1 2 2.
> Harmonic minor: 2 1 2 2 1 3 1 (raised 7th).
> Melodic minor going up: 2 1 2 2 2 2 1 (raised 6th and 7th).
> Modes in half steps: Ionian 2 2 1 2 2 2 1, Dorian 2 1 2 2 2 1 2, Phrygian 1 2 2 2 1 2 2, Lydian 2 2 2 1 2 2 1, Mixolydian 2 2 1 2 2 1 2, Aeolian 2 1 2 2 1 2 2, Locrian 1 2 2 1 2 2 2.
> Modes of C major, each starting on the next white key: Ionian from C, Dorian from D, Phrygian from E, Lydian from F, Mixolydian from G, Aeolian from A, Locrian from B.

Exercise: pick a home note or press Random, choose a mode or a minor scale from this page, and find its notes on the keys. The staff fills in as you go, and a wrong tap costs you a try. The melodic minor is built in its ascending form, so no coming down.
