This section holds scale-family theory: how scales are built from interval patterns, and the exercises that let you hear and construct them yourself rather than just read about them.

A **scale** is an ordered sequence of musical pitches — a ladder of notes spanning an octave that a piece of music draws its melody and harmony from. Every scale is anchored to a **tonic**, the note that feels like "home": a scale begins on its tonic and climbs step by step until it reaches the tonic again, an octave higher. Naming a scale means naming its tonic and its type — "C major" is the major scale whose tonic is C.

What defines a scale is its **pattern of intervals** — the whole (W) and half (H) steps between consecutive notes, not the specific letters:

- **Major** — W‑W‑H‑W‑W‑W‑H. Bright, resolved.
- **Natural minor** — W‑H‑W‑W‑H‑W‑W. Darker, more wistful.

Because the pattern is what matters, the same pattern started on a different tonic gives the same kind of scale in a new key — the notes change, the shape stays.
