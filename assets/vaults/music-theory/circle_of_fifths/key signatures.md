Every step clockwise adds one sharp. Every step counter-clockwise from C adds one flat. G has one sharp, F♯. D has two, F♯ and C♯. Keep walking and F♯ major has six. Go the other way: F has one flat, B♭. Then B♭ major has two (B♭ E♭), E♭ three, A♭ four, D♭ five, G♭ six.

The sharps always show up in the same order: F C G D A E B. That's the circle read clockwise, starting from F instead of C. The flats come in the reverse order: B E A D G C F. Same list, backwards.

Why exactly one at a time? A new key starts on the old key's 5th note and raises the old key's 4th note by a half step. Go from C to G: the 4th of C is F, and it becomes F♯. Everything else stays put. One step, one new sharp.

Two tricks let you read a signature without a table. For sharps, the home note is a half step above the last sharp: F♯ and C♯, last sharp C♯, half step up is D. D major. For flats, the home note is the second-to-last flat: B♭ and E♭, second-to-last is B♭. B♭ major. The one exception is F major, which has a single flat and no second-to-last. Just remember it.

Use the circle, not a table. Tables are for people who don't have one.
