---
description: "Use the wheel six ways: read a signature, find a minor twin, pick the I, IV and V, change key, follow a progression, recall the order."
---
The circle isn't a poster. It's a tool, and you'll reach for it six ways.

**Read a key signature.** From C, each step clockwise adds one sharp: G has one, D two, A three, E four, B five, F♯ six. Each step counter-clockwise adds one flat: F has one, B♭ two, E♭ three, A♭ four, D♭ five, G♭ six. Count the steps, done. [[key signatures]] has the trick.

**Find the minor twin.** Every major key has a minor key with the same signature, a minor third below: C and A minor, G and E minor. [[relative minors]] has the rest.

**Pick the three chords of a key.** Take a major key. The key one step counter-clockwise (its IV) and the key one step clockwise (its V) sit on either side of it: in C, F and G flank C. The three neighbours are the I, IV and V.

**Change key smoothly.** Neighbours share six of their seven notes: C major and G major differ only by F versus F♯. Move to a neighbour and nobody hears the seam. [[which keys are close]] explains why.

**Follow a progression.** Chord roots that move down a fifth, counter-clockwise on the wheel, are a very common motion in tonal music. The ii–V–I in C is D minor, G, C: each root sits one step counter-clockwise from the one before.

**Remember the order.** The sharps arrive F C G D A E B. The flats arrive in the reverse order, B E A D G C F.

Ready to drill it? [[practicing the circle]] has the exercises.
