---
description: "Find the minor twin of any major key, the one with the same notes, by climbing to the 6th note or dropping three half steps."
---
Every major key has a minor key that uses exactly the same notes. It's a twin with a different home.

The minor key starts on the 6th note of the major, which is a minor third (three half steps) below the major's home note. C major's 6th note is A, so A minor is its relative: C D E F G A B, just starting from A instead. Same signature, different centre, different mood.

On the wheel, the minor sits on the inner ring, right under its major. Four pairs to start with: C major and A minor, G major and E minor, D major and B minor, A major and F♯ minor. Look at how each minor lands on the 6th note of its major and you'll see the pattern.

To find the relative minor of any key, count down three half steps from its home note, or climb to the 6th note of its scale. Either way works. Pick whichever your fingers like.

Want the minor recipe on its own? That's [[natural minor scale]].

Try it: you get a signature on the staff, and you say which major and which minor it belongs to.
