In South India, drummers don't read notation. They **say** the rhythm. Five syllables: *tha, dhi, gi, na, thom*. Five beats. You speak it, then you play it, and when it's in your mouth it's in your hands. It's the oldest and best memory system for rhythm anyone has built.

This is the Carnatic tradition's way into five. Most Western ears count in fours; five feels like a stair that's one step longer than your leg expects.

The grid below is a stylised kit rendition: a high conga for the four opening syllables, a low conga where *thom* lands. It's the drums standing in for the voice.

Say it as you listen: *tha dhi gi na thom, tha dhi gi na thom.* If your mouth gets it before your head does, that's the system working.

## Challenge

Hit *thom* **twice**. Add a second low hit on the step right after the first. Say it aloud as *tha dhi gi na thom-thom*. You've just lengthened the phrase without changing the meter, which is exactly what a Carnatic drummer does when he's showing off.

Then shift the whole pattern one step to the right and see which beat the music now thinks is home.
