Two against three. Say it out loud and it sounds like homework. Play it and it's a small act of vandalism: the same bar of time, cut up two ways at once, and your brain can't pick a side.

The grid below has twelve steps. The hi-hat cuts them in **three** groups of four. The kick cuts them in **two** groups of six. They agree on the first step and then spend the bar pulling in different directions.

```forge-rhythm
[[rhythm_pattern_hemiola]]
```

Composers have used it for centuries to wreck a perfectly good waltz. It's the sudden lurch at the end of a phrase when 3/4 pretends to be 6/8. Bernstein's *America* is built on it.

## Challenge

Mute the hi-hat and listen to the kick alone. It's a plain, boring two. Now bring the hi-hat back and try to follow only the kick. Hard, right?

Now change the kick: instead of steps 0 and 6, put it on 0 and 4 and 8 (three, matching the hi-hat). Everything agrees, and everything is dull. **Disagreement is the point.** Find the smallest change that keeps the tension alive.
